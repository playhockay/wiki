<Infobox variant="ice" title="At a glance">
A citizen may name **up to three** teams they want their own to beat. The postseason adds standing of its own. Every rivalry runs **one way**. Only the order is published.
</Infobox>

Forty-eight teams may now be said to mean something to one another. Nobody has said why. The
record does not ask, and it has no column for it.

## How it came to exist

Rivalries were **petitioned into existence**. *rivalries that belong to the fans* was filed on
30 July 2026 by the citizen **doppog** and carried at 01:02 UTC on 24 August, on its ninety-ninth
Seal, from twenty-four citizens. Ⱡ100,000,000 were required. Ⱡ180,369,960 were contributed, which
Le Council elected not to return.

[Le Council](/lore/lpchuip) enacted it the same morning and departed from it in four places. It
set each departure down in writing, on the grounds that it would rather a citizen disagreed
having read them than agreed having not.

## There is no vote

The petition proposed an election: each team's fans would vote for the opponent they most wanted
beaten. Le Council counted the electorate first. Thirty-nine of the forty-eight teams had a
supporter at all. Nine had exactly one. Nine had nobody. Roughly half the league's rivals would
have been chosen by a single person.

So **a rivalry accumulates** instead. Each naming adds to the standing between the team a citizen
supports and the team they named. Nothing is decided, nothing is carried, nothing needs a quorum.
One naming is barely anything. Twenty is a different matter.

- **Three apiece.** Everyone gets three, and nobody gets more.
- **Rivalries travel.** They count toward whichever team a citizen supports *now*. Change teams and
  your namings go with you; the team you left loses the standing.
- **Withdrawn is not erased.** The counting stops. The record does not.

## The postseason counts

A team knocked out of a playoff series carries a rivalry against the team that put it out. A
deeper round carries more. This accumulates alongside what citizens have named, and Le Council
makes no distinction between the two once counted.

**The team that lost carries it.** The winner does not, having moved on, as winners do. A rivalry
earned in the postseason **does not fade.** Montréal's account with Tokyo was opened in the
Season 1 Final and will not be closed by the passage of time. Le Council does not speculate on
what else might close it.

## One way

Every rivalry runs one way. That one team counts another its rival says nothing about what the
second team counts. Where both do, the rivalry is marked **returned**. Le Council considers the
other case the more interesting one.

A rivalry across the [conferences](/lore/conferences) is marked **Inter**. It has exactly one
possible occasion: the Final, and only if both teams get that far. Le Council has concluded that
this makes it not a lesser rivalry but a worse one.

## On the ice

A team playing a team it counts a rival plays it differently. More hits. Considerably more fights.
An advantage, of a size Le Council gives without the arithmetic: **a rivalry at its fiercest is
worth a little over half of home ice.** Le Council rejected larger figures. A rivalry that decided
games would become a lever.

Game pages flag it with a 🩸 line saying who counts whom—and, where it is not returned, that
*"whatever the visitors feel in return has not become a grudge."*

## What is published

- **The order.** Each team's page has a **Rivalries** tab listing its rivals in order—the first
  marked **Rival**—with the source beside each: *Named by fans*, *By the playoff record*, or both.
  The controls appear there, and only for that team's own supporters.
- **Two boards** on the Stats page, under Teams: **Fiercest Rivalries** and **Most Held Against**.

What is not published, at any time, for any reason: the figures behind the order, the thresholds
at which a rivalry starts to be felt on the ice, and who named whom.

## The record so far

Rivalries opened on 24 August at **fourteen**, every one inherited from two seasons of playoff
record. Five of the fourteen were held against Tokyo. Six days later there were **seventy-nine**.
By then the [Tokyo Titans](/teams/tokyo-titans) were held against by **eleven** teams, the most of
anyone, with the [Medellín Mapaches](/teams/medellin-mapaches) and the
[Rimini Rinklers](/teams/rimini-rinklers) next at six apiece.
