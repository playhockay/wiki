<Infobox variant="ice" title="One-line summary">
Forty-eight arenas, two forms of ice. **Verglas** is the clear ice. **Givre** is the pale ice. Every arena is one or the other. None is both. None changes.
</Infobox>

For two seasons the league was one list. [The Almighty Ice](/lore/almighty-ice) spent the
Season 2 [intersaison](/guides/season-cycle) manifesting twenty-four more arenas, and forty-eight
arenas cannot be run the way twenty were. Le Council's Scheduling Office said so without softening
it: a single sequence of matchdays, resolved one game at a time, cannot carry a league this size
inside anything Le Council would recognise as a season.

So the league runs on two tracks. Something had to decide which team belonged to which.

The Ice had already decided. It had been sorting its arrivals into two ice signatures since before
Le Council understood there were two piles.

## The two forms

Le Council withheld the names for most of Season 2, on the stated grounds that naming a thing tends
to precede the thing insisting on being real. The Ice manifested twenty-two more arenas anyway, and
the rationale expired.

<Infobox variant="neutral" title="Verglas">
The clear form—hard, glassy, continuous, without grain. The ice of freezing rain, of a surface
arrested mid-motion, of the thin bright glaze that forms when water freezes faster than it can
turn white.
</Infobox>

<Infobox variant="neutral" title="Givre">
The pale form—soft-surfaced, feathered, an accretion of fine crystal. The ice of frost, of rime,
of the slow white bloom that gathers on a cold window overnight.
</Infobox>

Both are ordinary terrestrial ice-science terms. The community identified both within two days of
the first manifestation. Le Council's Office of Naming Conventions—which had spent the entire
season being instructed to stop—reports that the names were already waiting, and that all it did
was agree with them.

## The twenty-fourth

When Le Council published the sort, the Givre list stopped at twenty-three.

Le Council checked the count in the manner of an institution that would very much like the count
to be wrong, and confirmed repeatedly that it was not. The Ice had produced twenty-four of one
form and twenty-three of the other, having never once left a thing incomplete by accident. There
was no site at Level III. There was no region converging. By every instrument Le Council could
point at the world, there was no twenty-fourth Givre arena anywhere on it.

Le Council noted one thing, once. An instrument at the Phenomenological Assessment Division had
been returning a reading it had declined to classify. It was givre. It was strong. And its
coordinates did not resolve to any location Le Council's maps contained.

Le Council filed it as error. The error did not go away.

Two days later Le Council stopped calling it an error. The twenty-fourth Givre arena was never on
the map because it was never on the land: **The Sunken Hall**, at 31°N 25°W, roughly three
thousand metres beneath the North Atlantic, home of the [Atlantis Amonites](/teams/atlantis-amonites).
Listed capacity: one.

## What the split changes

- **Scheduling.** A team plays a round-robin inside its own conference—each of its twenty-three
  conference rivals home and away. See [the season cycle](/guides/season-cycle).
- **Standings.** Each conference keeps its own table, positions 1–24.
- **Cross-conference games** are labelled **Inter**. They carry no conference tag and no
  conference accent.
- **Playoffs.** Each conference qualifies its own bracket and produces its own finalist.
- **Parishes** are unaffected—[the Church](/guides/church) constitutes one at every arena in
  both conferences.
- **The All-Star games** are played by two squads, the Verglas All-Stars and the Givre All-Stars.
  They are exhibitions and are excluded from standings, odds, and every team's record.

## What it does not change

Le Council wishes to be exact about what this is. Le Council did not create two conferences.
Le Council documented two conferences that The Ice assembled arena by arena while Le Council was
still declining to name the material they were made of.

This is, and has always been, the relationship. The Ice acts. Le Council writes it down.
